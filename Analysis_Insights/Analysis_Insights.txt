SUPERSTORE SALES ANALYSIS - KEY QUESTIONS & INSIGHTS
======================================================

1. QUESTION: Which product category/sub-category generates the most profit
   — and are any actually losing money?

   INSIGHT: All three categories generate similar total profit (Office
   Supplies and Furniture ~755K, Technology ~730K) — no category is
   dramatically outperforming the others. At the sub-category level,
   Tables and Chairs are the most profitable (~195K each), while
   Computers and Storage are the least profitable (~130-135K). No
   sub-category shows a net loss in this dataset — every sub-category
   is profitable, just at varying levels.


2. QUESTION: How do sales trend over time — are there seasonal spikes
   (e.g., holiday months, Q4)?

   INSIGHT: Monthly sales fluctuate between ~440K and ~595K over the
   two-year period (May 2023-March 2025) with no strong or consistent
   seasonal pattern — there's no clear Q4/holiday spike. Peaks and dips
   appear irregular rather than tied to a specific season, suggesting
   demand in this dataset isn't strongly seasonal. (Note: incomplete
   boundary months at the start and end of the dataset were excluded
   to avoid distorting the trend line.)


3. QUESTION: Which region or state performs best/worst in sales and
   profit?

   INSIGHT: South leads in both sales (~3.25M) and profit (~575K),
   followed closely by West, Central, and East. However, the gap
   between regions is small, and all four regions convert sales to
   profit at a similar rate (~17-18% margin) — meaning no region is
   significantly underperforming or over-relying on discounting to
   drive volume.


4. QUESTION: Who are the top 10 customers by revenue, and what % of
   total sales do they represent?

   INSIGHT: The top 10 customers (led by Michael Williams at $13,947
   and Michael Smith at $12,879) collectively generated approximately
   $101,646 in sales — roughly 0.8% of total company revenue (~$12.5M).
   This low concentration is a positive sign: the business isn't
   overly dependent on a small handful of customers, which reduces
   revenue risk compared to a business where top customers might
   represent 20-30%+ of sales.


5. QUESTION: Does higher discount lead to lower profit? (i.e., is heavy
   discounting hurting the business?)

   INSIGHT: No, not in this dataset. Testing both raw profit and
   profit margin, correlation with discount rate was close to 0
   (weak, roughly -0.05 to -0.15) even after removing unrealistic
   outlier margins. Profit margins stayed similarly spread (roughly
   0-20% average) at both low and high discount levels, indicating
   discount level alone is not a reliable driver of profitability
   here — other factors like product category likely play a bigger
   role.


6. QUESTION: Which customer segment (Consumer, Corporate, Home Office)
   is the most profitable, and how does their average order value
   compare?

   INSIGHT: Home Office is the most profitable segment (~775K total
   profit), slightly ahead of Corporate and Consumer (both ~730K,
   nearly identical to each other). Despite likely having fewer total
   customers than Consumer, Home Office converts sales into profit
   more efficiently — worth a follow-up analysis on average order
   value or discount behavior by segment to pinpoint the exact driver.
